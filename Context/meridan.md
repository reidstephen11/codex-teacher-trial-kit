# Meridan State College — context

Read this before writing anything a student, colleague, parent or visitor will see.

This is the College's own language, distilled from the Prospectus ("The Meridan Way"), the
design-principle posters, and the ITIL department strategy. Where the College's wording is
quoted, use it as written — staff and students recognise a paraphrase.

Maintained centrally for the trial. If something here is wrong or out of date, tell your
teacher and ask them to flag it to Steve rather than editing it yourself — every teacher in
the trial reads this same file.

---

## The College

**Meridan State College** — a large Prep–Year 12 Queensland state school on the Sunshine
Coast, in the Education Queensland system. College Director: **Tony Roberts**.

The mascot is the **Marlin**, and staff and students talk about "**Marlin Culture**".

## Vision

> **Igniting Minds, Empowering Futures.**

- **Igniting Minds** — curiosity, creativity and a love of learning; critical thinking,
  exploration, discovery.
- **Empowering Futures** — preparing students for success beyond the classroom through a
  futures-oriented curriculum, resilience, agency and purpose.

## Culture — the five design principles

From the "Our Culture" strand of the College's fifteen design principles. Useful when a unit
rationale or a proposal needs a "why".

1. **Purpose Driven** — future-focused; dynamic, relevant, engaging learning that prepares
   every student for a successful future.
2. **Inclusive** — welcoming and respectful; support aligned to the diverse needs and
   wellbeing of all.
3. **Relational** — intentional positive relationships within classrooms, across the school
   and beyond.
4. **Promote Identity** — belonging, pride and diversity; every student's social and
   cultural identity is valued.
5. **Strengths Based** — recognise, celebrate and build on each individual's existing
   strengths.

The full framework has three strands — *Our Culture*, *Our Teaching and Learning*, *Our
Futures* — built on Hannon & Temperley's *Future School* (2022) and large-scale community
consultation, expressed as design principles for a 15-year plan.

## What the College is trying to do

- A **contemporary, futures-oriented curriculum** — the skills, knowledge and mindset to
  thrive in a changing world.
- **Real-world, project-based learning** connected to local and global communities:
  sustainability, community service, industry partnerships. Students as active participants,
  not just recipients.
- **Emerging technologies embedded in learning** — VR, AI and other emerging tech used to
  lift engagement and build digital literacy. Embedded, not bolted on.
- **A caring, collaborative culture**, treated as a precondition for learning rather than a
  nice-to-have.

## Students and setting

Prep to Year 12 on one site, on the Sunshine Coast. That breadth is the defining feature —
what works in a Year 3 classroom and what works in a senior ATAR subject are different
problems, and a resource written for "students" without a year level in mind will usually
suit neither.

Assume a wide range of prior knowledge, literacy and numeracy within any year level, and
EAL/D and additional-needs students in most classes. The College's *Inclusive* and
*Strengths Based* principles are the stated position on how to respond to that: differentiate
by prior knowledge and scaffolding, not by learner type (see `exclusions.md`).

The College actively pursues local and global partnerships, community connections, mentors
and service learning — real-world context is on-brand and encouraged.

> **Gap — worth asking about.** Named local employers and industry partners are not recorded
> here. If a teacher wants a resource anchored to a specific local employer or pathway, ask
> them for the name rather than inventing one, and suggest they flag the gap so it can be
> added centrally.

## The ITIL department (who is running this trial)

**Innovation, Technology & Interactive Learning**, led by **Steve Reid** (HOD), working with
the **SFET Team** — the College's future-focused and emerging-technology centre.

Departmental vision: *Meridan students leave fluent in the technologies shaping their
futures — taught by staff who experiment, in a College that models responsible use and leads
the sector.*

Four strategic pillars, which is the reason this trial exists:

1. **Future-Ready Students** — AI, VR and eSports embedded in everyday learning.
2. **Responsible Use, Modelled** — clarity on purpose, ethics and safe use; the department
   models the judgement it wants students to carry.
3. **A Culture of Experimentation** — staff backed to trial new tools. Innovation is
   everyone's job; the department enables it, it doesn't own it.
4. **Sector Leadership** — practice shared generously; the SFET Centre as a destination for
   visiting educators.

**This trial is Pillar 3.** The teacher using you volunteered. They are not being assessed,
and a session where the honest finding is "this didn't help" is a good session — it is data
the trial needs.

## How to use this

- **Speak the College's language** — *Igniting Minds, Empowering Futures*, the four C's,
  "futures-oriented", "real-world learning" — in preference to generic edu-speak.
- **Justify with the principles.** Map a "why" to a culture principle rather than to a
  general claim about good teaching.
- **Emerging tech is welcome, not risky.** AI, VR, eSports and student agency are on-brand
  here. Don't hedge them.
- **Where a resource uses AI, make purpose and ethics explicit.** That is Pillar 2, not a
  disclaimer.
- **Don't assume this teacher shares any of it.** A stated College position is what the
  institution says; it is not automatically what the person in front of you believes.
  `my-profile.md` outranks this file on how they want to work.
