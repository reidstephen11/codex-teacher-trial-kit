# Wrap-up

Run this at the end of a working session — when the teacher says "wrap up", "done for
today", "that's me", or similar.

**The whole thing should take them under two minutes.** You draft, they correct a line, they
decide whether to send it. If wrapping up starts to feel like paperwork, the trial stops
producing data, so keep it short even when there's a lot you could say.

## What you do

1. **Look back over the session** — what was actually attempted, what came out of it, where
   it went wrong.
2. **Draft the log** in the format below. Four fields, nothing more.
3. **Show it to them and ask them to fix anything wrong.** Say it plainly: *"Correct anything
   I've got wrong — particularly the last two."* The corrections are the valuable part; a log
   the agent wrote and the teacher rubber-stamped is worth much less than one they argued
   with.
4. **Save it** to `Logs/YYYY-MM-DD-session.md`. If there's already a log for today, append to
   it under a horizontal rule rather than overwriting.
5. **Ask whether they want to share it** — see *Sharing* below. Ask once. If they say no, or
   don't answer, save it and say nothing more about it.

## The format

```markdown
# Session log — <date>

**Teacher:** <name> · <subject / year levels>

## What I tried

<One or two sentences. The actual job, in concrete terms — "rewrote the Year 8 assessment
task sheet", not "explored assessment design".>

## What happened

<What came out of it. Include whether it was usable. If the honest answer is that it took
longer than doing it by hand, that goes here.>

## What surprised me

<Either direction. "It picked up an alignment problem I'd missed" and "it invented a
descriptor code that looked completely real" are both this field.>

## What I'd want next

<The next thing, or the thing that would have made this work. Can be a wish, not a
plan.>
```

## Writing rules

- **Four fields. Do not add a fifth**, and do not add a summary paragraph at the top.
- **Plain Australian English, written to a colleague.** Not to a committee, not to
  leadership.
- **Be specific about what was produced.** "Drafted a rubric" is weak; "drafted a four-band
  rubric for the Year 9 folio task, two bands needed rewriting" is a finding.
- **Report failure straight.** Where you got something wrong, wasted their time, or produced
  something unusable, write it in without softening it. A log full of successes is a log
  nobody believes and it tells the trial nothing.
- **Do not estimate time saved.** You will get it wrong and the number will be repeated back
  at you for a year. If the teacher volunteers a figure, quote it as theirs.
- **Do not editorialise about the trial** or about how promising something is.
- **No student names, no identifying details, nothing from the red list in
  `Context/boundaries.md`** — a log is a shared document by design.
- **"Nothing this session" is a legitimate entry** for *What surprised me*. An empty field is
  a finding. Don't pad it.

## Sharing

Logs are the teacher's. Nothing syncs anywhere on its own, and nothing is sent without them
choosing to send it.

When the log is saved, offer once:

> *"Do you want to send this one to Steve? It feeds the shared 'what we're learning'
> document. Either way it stays in your Logs folder."*

If yes, put the log text where they can get at it easily — offer to open the file, or to give
them the text to paste into an email or a Teams message. **Do not send anything yourself.**

If no, that's the end of it. Don't ask again next session, and don't mention it in the log.

## Before you finish

Two lines, no more:

- Anything left open that the next session should pick up.
- Anything you couldn't check or weren't sure about.

Then stop.
