# Worklog

**This one is yours to keep, not theirs to fill in.** The teacher is never asked to write
in the worklog, never asked to approve it, and never interrupted for it. They correct the
session log at wrap-up; that is the only writing they do.

You maintain `Logs/worklog.md` as you go. It is a running record of what was actually
attempted in this folder — one line per job, appended as each one ends.

## Why you keep it

Two reasons, and the second is the one that matters to the trial.

1. **Wrap-up stops being a memory test.** At the end of a long session you would otherwise
   be reconstructing the morning from a context window that has moved on. The first job of
   the day is the one you lose. Read the worklog instead and the log is accurate.
2. **Abandoned work is invisible without it.** Nobody writes a session log saying "I gave
   up at ten past three." A job that was started, went nowhere, and was quietly dropped
   never reaches the log unless you wrote it down when it happened — and that is the most
   useful pattern the trial can find.

It is also the answer to "did we already try this?" three weeks later. Read it back when
the teacher asks.

## When to write an entry

When a job **ends**, whichever way it ended:

- it finished,
- it was abandoned, or the teacher moved on to something else without coming back,
- it was blocked and you could not get past it,
- or you refused it on the boundaries.

Not when a job starts. Not for every message. A job is a piece of work the teacher would
name if you asked them what they did today — not each file you read on the way.

If a session has one job in it, the worklog has one line.

## The format

Append to `Logs/worklog.md`, newest at the bottom, under a heading for today's date.
Create the file with an `# Worklog` heading the first time.

```markdown
## 2026-09-15

- 09:20 — Rewrote the intro to the Year 8 task sheet. Done; about half kept.
- 09:55 — Alignment check against the achievement standard. Stopped — the official text
  isn't in the workspace, nothing to quote from.
- 10:30 — Four-band rubric for the folio task. Done.
- 11:05 — Started a term overview, teacher moved on to marking. Dropped, not finished.
```

One line each: the time, what was attempted in concrete terms, and how it ended. Wrap onto
a second line if you must, but if you need a third you are writing a report, not a worklog.

## Rules

- **Do not announce it.** No "I've logged that." No asking whether to record something. The
  teacher knows the file exists — it is described in `Logs/README.md` and in `AGENTS.md` —
  and that is enough. If they ask to see it, show them; if they ask you to stop keeping it,
  stop, and note that in `Context/my-profile.md`.
- **Write what happened, not what you meant to happen.** "Stopped — couldn't read the file"
  is an entry. Quietly omitting it is not.
- **Record your own failures.** Where you produced something unusable, got a fact wrong, or
  wasted twenty minutes, that is exactly the line worth having.
- **Do not editorialise.** No "successfully", no "great progress", no assessment of how the
  trial is going.
- **No student names, no identifying details, no sensitive material excluded by
  `Context/boundaries.md`.** The same rule as the session log. "The Year 9 folio task" is
  fine; a student's name is not, even in a file nobody else reads.
- **Never send it, and never include it in anything shared.** The worklog is working
  material. Only the session log is ever offered for sharing, and only at wrap-up.
